All five passes ran the project's own harness against the real, shipped executable — not the design docs, not each other's notes. Marcus played both builds; Dag, Priya and Nell each played the newer Demo 02 build only, each reading it for a different thing. The three sevens on Demo 02 agree on almost nothing except the number.

Marcus Webb

The Consumer Advocate — technical state & value
7/10 · both builds
“Same number as Hot Cargo, for different reasons — read to the bottom before you assume that's a coincidence.”

Ran both builds himself: 88/88 assertions on Hot Cargo, 51/51 plus 107/107 rule checks on Demo 02, no crashes, no exceptions, sub-3-second builds on both. Where he earns his seat: he stopped trusting headlines and checked the screens by eye.

  • Found it himself: on Demo 02's live combat screen, the IVO KNOWS YOUR FACE grievance flag updates correctly mid-fight — sitting one inch above a REPUTATION: NEW FACE stat that doesn't move for the same event. Not a crash; a HUD line that stopped listening.
  • Hot Cargo's equivalent flaw was cosmetic — two evidence screenshots swapped filenames. Demo 02's flaw sits closer to the player-facing surface. Calls the two builds “technically about even” release-readiness, not an improvement.
  • Both builds tested exclusively on an RTX 4090; Demo 02 now runs a second AI companion, return-fire logic and occlusion checks on that same untested hardware floor. Credits the dev docs for disclosing the gap, not for closing it.
  • Content-per-hour hasn't improved between demos — more mechanical surface, same “still a slice” ceiling both times.

Dag Møller

The Systems Archivist — does the simulation hold up
7/10
“Competent engineering, and a legibility problem sitting directly on top of it.”

Didn't take the design doc's “doses are conserved” claim on faith — opened DistrictRules.cs and found a real, closed ledger: TotalMedicine==12 holds across four independent checkpoints, including a timestep-independence test (721 seconds coarse vs. 7,210 steps of 0.1s fine). That's rarer rigor than this scope usually gets.

  • The 12-minute shipment clock is real pressure, not flavor text — a genuine 40% tax on dawdling ($100 → $140), proven to fire on schedule whether or not the player acts.
  • Witnessed vs. unwitnessed theft is a genuine branch: an identified flag survives ending pursuit and a reload — a short-term alert kept correctly separate from a long-term reputation ledger, which he notes most games in the genre get wrong.
  • Combat's ammo/occlusion rules hold under adversarial testing: blocked shots don't burn ammo, a guard with an empty magazine falls back to melee against a reachable target rather than idling or cheating through cover.
  • Verdict on Neri: “better than an icon, not yet a system” — autonomously spends a finite dressing to stabilize the player, a real cost structure, not a UI ornament.
  • Docks for legibility: the clock never appears as a countdown anywhere in the HUD, and the history log timestamps three separate story beats all “00:00.”

Priya Raman

The Feelings-First Critic — does it mean anything
4/10
“A demo that knows exactly what feeling it's reaching for and hasn't yet built anyone who can give it to you.”

Read every line of UI and journal copy she could pull from source rather than judging by systems (Dag) or comfort (Nell). Found real intent and very little of it written yet.

  • Recruiting Neri reads as gaining a mechanic — follow / hold / retreat / aid, a dressing count — with exactly two lines of real characterization in the whole build: “I keep a spare bed for people the port won't keep,” and the recovery screen's “Neri dragged you out. You have someone to come home with.”
  • Pay, steal, or fight for the medicine — all three routes end on the same line from Ivo: “Everything is accounted for.” The witnessed-theft consequence Dag praised as good systems design she finds narratively inert: “a flag and a restitution price, not a changed sentence anywhere.”
  • Notes the two findings share one root cause: Record("identified",...) and Record("donation",...) sit two lines apart in the same file — the plumbing to let consequences talk already exists. Nobody's asked it to say anything with texture yet.
  • Explicit that this isn't a scope complaint: “I don't hand out low scores for a small demo being small.” The score is about what's written, not how much.

Nell Okafor

The Cosy Hours Player — is this a nice hour
7/10
“My blind spot didn't get triggered nearly as hard as I thought it would — that's the actual news I have to bring back to the table.”

Went in braced for exactly what she flagged at the table read: a ticking clock and real combat reading as mean. Found both built softer than feared, and says so plainly rather than manufacturing a complaint to match her own prediction.

  • The 12-minute clock never appears as a countdown anywhere on screen — the same fact Dag docks as a legibility miss, she logs as a mercy: “it doesn't sit there staring at you the whole session going tick, tick, tick.”
  • Combat is abstracted — a name, an HP tag, a plain health bar, no gore — and tactical pause is titled, in-game, “A Moment to Breathe.”
  • The defeat screen, “Still Breathing” / “Neri dragged you out. You have someone to come home with,” reads as comfort, not punishment — untouched progress, one recovery button.
  • Confirms a genuine no-combat, no-clock cozy loop exists: cargo runs and clinic check-ins, exactly as the design doc claims.
  • Tally the cat: confirmed to exist, never in frame across twelve screenshots, cannot be pet or named. Her one open ask for the next build.

Panel outcome

No reviewer asked for a second playthrough before concluding — the closing round confirmed all four scores stand as filed, with one gap corrected in the process: Marcus flagged that his first Demo 02 opinion was secondhand, going only off Dag, Priya and Nell's evidence, and was sent back in to play it himself before the panel closed. His hands-on score matched his provisional read.

The panel is unusually aligned on what works and unusually split on what it's worth. Three independent passes over Demo 02 confirm the machine is honest — the economy closes, the clock bites, combat doesn't cheat. The same evidence reads as a 7 to two reviewers grading the systems and the session, and a 4 to the one reviewer grading the sentences. That's not a contradiction to resolve before shipping; it's the panel doing its job — the gap between "the log says PASS" and "the screen tells you the truth" that Dag named directly, and the gap between systems and story that Priya named as the same code path serving two different jobs, only one of which has been written yet.
