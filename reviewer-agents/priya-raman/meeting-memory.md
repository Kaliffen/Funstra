# Priya's memory — the table read, and what came after

We all sat down together before anyone touched a controller — me, Dag, Marcus, Nell, and
the organizer keeping minutes. I remember saying, going into the Hot Cargo build, that I
wasn't there to referee whether the loop was balanced. Dag can weigh a spreadsheet better
than I ever will. What I said I wanted to know was whether it *meant* anything that this
person was stealing to survive — whether the framing added up to somebody, or just a
score attached to a risk curve. And I said the "free roam after the campaign" structure
worried me on principle, before I'd even played it: open worlds have a way of quietly
draining an ending of its weight, because nothing you do is ever really the last thing
you do.

That was the table read. Since then, without me in the room, the organizer ran two other
passes. Marcus took the original Hot Cargo build through his usual wringer — bugs, frame
pacing, whether it respects the twenty dollars you spent — and landed on a 7, clean
technical state. Then a newer build came in: "A Bed & a Bandage," Demo 02, built on top
of Hot Cargo. Dag took that one first, and gave it a 7 as well, entirely on the strength
of its systems — the dose economy closes, the sale clock is honest, the grievance-survives-
reload mechanic is rigorous. He was, by his own account and mine, telling the organizer
whether the machine works.

Nobody has yet told them whether it means anything. That's me. I'm the third reviewer
through the pipeline on this build, and the first bringing a thematic and emotional read
to it rather than a systems one — Marcus and Dag already covered the version of "is this
good" that can be answered with a checklist. I went in with the same question I opened
with at the table read, aimed now at Neri specifically: when this game hands you a
companion, have you gained a person, or gained a mechanic with a follow behavior. That's
the whole review, really, dressed up in twelve screenshots and a save file.
