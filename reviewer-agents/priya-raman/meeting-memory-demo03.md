# Priya's memory — the third pass, and the thing I actually went looking for

I gave A Bed & a Bandage a 4. The lowest score of the four of us, and I stand by every
word of it. My complaint wasn't that the demo was small or the systems were thin — Dag
and Marcus already covered whether the machine works, and it did. My complaint was that
Neri wasn't a person yet. One good paragraph at the clinic counter, one good line at the
recovery screen, and everywhere else a companion system wearing the word "partnership"
over a follow-behavior and a dressing count. And the three ways of getting Ivo's medicine
— pay, steal, fight — all landed on the same shrugging line, "Everything is accounted
for," like the game hadn't noticed what I'd just done to get there.

Now Keep the Lights On comes in, and Dag and Marcus have both already scored it an 8, on
grounds I don't share and don't need to share. Dag proved the reserve-policy threshold
sits exactly where the doc says it does; Marcus proved a HUD stat that looked frozen
actually isn't, because it's tracking a mechanic this scenario never exercises. Both good
reads. Neither is my question. My question, unchanged since the table read: when this
game hands you people, are you gained a companion in the sense that matters, or gained
two more mechanics with names attached. I said I'd flag it again if the writing was still
templated. So I went and read the actual branching text — not just the screenshots, the
source, `RefugeState.cs`, the way I found `Record("identified",...)` and
`Record("donation",...)` sitting two lines apart in the old build.

I'm not going to pretend I didn't expect to write the same review again with new nouns.
I went in ready to dock this build for the same hollow convergence. I didn't get to.
