# A Bed & a Bandage — Nell's playtest review

**Verdict: 7/10.** Would I put this on after a bad day? Mostly yes — the town, the
pace, and the caretaking loop with Neri, absolutely. The clinic-and-collector plot
itself, only on a day I actually have a bit of nerve in me. That's not nothing,
that's genuinely a "yes, with a caveat I didn't expect to be this small."

## What I actually looked at

I'm not going to pretend I audited the dose economy or read every line of dialogue
for depth — that's Dag and Priya's jobs and they already did them (7/10 and 4/10,
more on that below). I ran the actual test harness (`Test-Demo.ps1 -Mode Full
-Visible`) from a clean checkout, watched it drive a real character through the
whole collector/clinic/companion/combat loop, and it came back with a clean sweep
— fifty-one PASS lines, nothing red, nothing skipped. Then I sat with the twelve
screenshots it dropped in `Evidence\` (B01 through B12, timestamped from my own
run this evening, not leftovers from anyone else's pass) and looked at them the
way I look at anything: does this town look like somewhere I want to be at
9:40pm, and does the screen ever shout at me.

## The town, first

Old Port at dusk is genuinely lovely, honestly. Warm building colours — rust,
sage, plum — soft yellow window lights, a couple of cars idling, someone's laundry
sign lit up on a corner. The title screen alone ("One person. A city of debts.
Someone to trust.") reads more melancholy-cosy than grim-cosy, which is a real
genre and I am extremely at home in it. This is not a neon-blood cyberpunk hellhole.
It's a quiet port town where everyone happens to owe somebody money. I'd walk
around in this for the atmosphere alone.

## The HUD — and here's where I got pleasantly surprised

I went in braced for a threat board. Countdown timer ticking somewhere, wanted
stars, ammo counter screaming at me. What I actually got, across all twelve
shots: a calm little stack of cards in the corner. Your name and status ("YOU /
STABLE," later "YOU / BLEEDING," which, fine, is a word, but it's typeset the same
size as everything else, not flashing). A health bar that's a plain teal-to-red
line, not a gore meter. Bandages, debt, ammo, all just numbers on dark cards. Cash
and reputation up top, a "KEEP A LOW PROFILE" bar that's honestly so understated
I almost missed it entirely on first pass.

Here's the thing I want to flag because it's exactly my known blind spot and I
said as much going in: **the twelve-minute shipment clock is not on screen
anywhere I could find.** No countdown widget, no timer bar ticking down in a
corner. It exists — the design doc is upfront that after twelve minutes of active
play the shipment gets sold and costs more to get back — but you would not feel
it pressing on you the way you'd feel, say, a wanted-star system flashing red. It's
pressure that lives in the world's clock, not in your face. For someone like me
who bounces off exactly that kind of visible dread, this matters a lot: I was
worried the demo would put a ticking bomb in my HUD and instead it just quietly
lets the appointment happen off to the side if you dawdle. You can absolutely
still get caught out by it — that's a real cost, not a fake one — but it doesn't
sit there staring at you the whole session going tick, tick, tick. I appreciate
that more than I expected to.

## Combat — abstracted enough that I didn't flinch

B05 and B06 are the actual fight screens and I made myself look properly instead
of skimming past. It's a small highlighted circle on a person, a name-and-HP tag
("ROOK / 80 HP"), your own health ticking down and a red "BLEEDING" label. No
blood spray, no ragdoll, no camera shake, nothing that reads as violent so much as
a boardgame combat resolution laid over a nice little isometric street. Tactical
pause (B06, "A MOMENT TO BREATHE" is literally the pause screen's own title, I am
not making that up) lets you stop, look at the board, and think, rather than
mashing buttons in a panic. That's exactly the kind of thing that keeps a fight
from feeling mean to me — I can treat it like a puzzle instead of a jump-scare.

## Getting hurt, and the recovery screen — genuinely the nicest surprise of the whole pass

This is the bit I want Priya and Dag to both notice, because I don't think either
of them was grading on this axis. The defeat screen (B10) is titled "STILL
BREATHING" and the actual body text is: *"Neri dragged you out. You have someone
to come home with."* That is not a punishment screen. That is a hug with a health
readout attached. You lose some health, the shipment stays with whoever had it,
and your progress and gear are untouched. One button: "GET BACK ON YOUR FEET."
The home/rest screen is titled "A BED TO COME BACK TO," and even the framing
line under it — "Shelter, not immunity" — is honest without being cruel about it.
The doc backs this up too: a nearby Neri improves your recovery and skips the
worse debt, and even penniless after a bad run there's still a credit-funded bed
waiting for you. I went in expecting defeat to feel like a slap. It felt like
someone putting a blanket on me. I did not expect to type that sentence about a
demo with a gun in it, but here we are.

## Neri — yes, this is the caretaking loop I wanted

The partnership screen is called "NOT ALONE ANYMORE" and the actual instruction
text says "equip, protect and care for each other," which, come on, that's
practically Stardew dialogue. Neri follows you, can be told to hold or retreat,
has their own limited dressings, stabilizes you when you're down, and — this is
the part that matters to me — you bring dressings back to *them* if they get hurt.
That's a two-way little chore loop, not a one-way babysitting job. It didn't read
as an added responsibility stacked on top of my evening. It read as the reason to
have an evening. My own Stardew farm has had a paid-off mortgage for four
in-game years and I still check on the chickens every morning for no reason, so I
know a good little check-in loop when I see one, and this is one.

## Can I just... hang out?

Yes, actually, and I went looking for exactly this. The doc says plainly that if
you don't touch the medicine plot, "the solo life and the original activities
remain playable" — Mara's jobs, the repeatable purple cargo runs, banking loads
at home. B12 shows the home-banking loop in action (hold E, bank the haul, no
combat, no clock, no collector anywhere in frame) and it's just... a nice little
errand. Walk somewhere, carry a thing, bring it home, watch a number go up. I
could absolutely spend a whole session never going near Ivo or Rook at all and
just do cargo runs and clinic check-ins, and that session would be calm start to
finish.

## The cat

Resolved, mostly. Tally exists — the doc says so explicitly, "a small cat, Tally,
asleep at the clinic," ambient scenery. I did not catch Tally in frame in any of
the twelve harness screenshots (the clinic interior close-ups the scenario grabs
don't happen to land on the cat), so I can't show you a picture of my new
best friend, but I believe the paperwork. You cannot pet Tally, you cannot name
Tally, Tally is a sleeping shape and nothing more. So: there is a cat, which is
the thing I actually asked for at the table read, and I will take partial credit
for making them confirm it exists. But it's furniture, not a pet, and that's a
small real disappointment for someone whose whole thing is "anything you can name
and keep." Next demo, let me feed the cat. That's genuinely my top note.

## Where I differ from Dag and from Priya

Dag gave this 7/10 on whether the systems hold — closed dose loop, a clock that
means something, combat rules that behave. I'm not touching that, he's right,
the harness ran fifty-one green checks in front of me and I believe him completely.
Priya gave this 4/10 on whether it *means* anything, and thought the pay/steal/
fight choice has no emotional weight and Neri mostly reads as a mechanic with a
couple of good lines. I don't disagree with what she saw, but I'm not scoring on
that axis at all — I don't need the choice to be profound, I need the session to
be pleasant, and on that separate question the game does something neither of
them was measuring: it took the two things I was most scared of going in — a
ticking clock and real combat with a defeat state — and built both of them softer
than I expected. The clock hides itself instead of nagging me. The defeat screen
comforts instead of scolds. That's the actual news I have to bring back to the
table: my blind spot didn't get triggered nearly as hard as I thought it would,
and that's worth more to my score than either the systems rigor or the missing
emotional weight.

## Would I put this on after a bad day?

For the cargo-and-clinic-checkins version of this evening, genuinely yes, no
hesitation — warm town, small errands, a companion worth checking on, a pause
screen that tells me to breathe. For the version where I actually go pick a fight
with Rook over the medicine, only on a day I've got a little steel in me, because
even a soft-pedalled defeat screen is still a defeat screen and I'd rather not
risk it when I'm already having a rough one. The good news, genuinely, is that
the game itself agrees with me and lets me skip that whole wing of the building
if I want to. That's the review, really: it's kinder to a nervous player than it
looks on paper, and it lets you leave the scary door shut without punishing you
for it. 7/10, and I'd take the update that lets me feed the cat.
