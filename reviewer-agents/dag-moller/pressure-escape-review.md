## Review

The previous build had nine people on its ledger and a short example of an officer shooting. This one has nine people spending ammunition in the same running encounter. My stress pass recorded nine shooters, eight reloaders and seven empty inventories by the end, with 22 rounds left across the roster. The trucks arrived while combat and traffic continued. These are useful interacting limits: a response takes time to assemble, has to find somewhere to unload, and cannot quietly manufacture another magazine when the arithmetic becomes inconvenient. It now earns the claim of collective activity that I withheld last time.

The shape of the decision is also demonstrated. Leaving the prepared normal-health player exposed produces an actual defeat. Moving immediately from the same prepared position around successive corners produces a surviving player, a SEARCH state, a saved and reloaded pursuit, and eventual safety. The surviving player returns to the clinic under the controller rather than appearing there because the fixture calls recovery. That gives buildings a concrete job beyond supplying a decorative street plan. It is the sort of modest systemic connection that should be established before multiplying the number of weapons.

I particularly approve of what the pursuit does not forget and what it does stop pretending to know. During the inspected post-reload samples, the player is near x=-45 while the remembered sighting remains near x=35. Nine responding officers remain in the save; their report does not follow the concealed player across the town. SEARCH is visible in the map frame, and the end notification says that immediate pursuit has ended while recorded incidents remain. The distinction is legible without opening source code. A last-reported-area cue or an indication of search progress could make the waiting period more informative, but the old interface's bare headcount has materially improved.

There is an important limitation in the successful withdrawal. It ends at 100 health, and all 18 post-reload samples retain the full 162 police rounds. The conditional bandage check did not execute. This demonstrates that prompt withdrawal from the edge of the prepared firing lane can work; it does not tell me how late I may recognize my mistake, whether I can recover from a wound in the alley, or whether another nearby route leaves a useful margin. Exposure versus immediate retreat is a sound first comparison. It is still two ends of a decision, with little evidence about the middle where most interesting play would occur.

Nor should the stress profile be sold as disciplined formation tactics. Nine living officers first appear in the sampled profile at about 25 seconds; the sampled count falls to eight at about 37 seconds and finishes at seven. Two police records reach zero health despite the player not continuing to fire after the resident-trigger setup. Source inspection permits police projectiles to hit other actors and shows only a current-line firing check, so friendly fire is a plausible explanation; this run does not attribute each damaging projectile. Several empty officers end up close to the stationary, artificially durable player. That is useful evidence of a response coming apart under its finite resources, but its significance for normal play remains uncertain because normal arrest is disabled in that section. I am recording an observed limit and a hypothesis, not announcing a reproduced ordinary-play exploit.

The tactical pause repair is straightforward and successful. Weapon, magazine and reserve occupy their own strip above the pause instructions. The map and notification no longer conceal that strip, and an unrecruited companion does not reserve a large disabled orders grid. E02 and E04 are readable at the tested resolution. The map itself still has competing labels around the collector/garage area; that is minor navigation friction, not the old ammunition obstruction. These are small presentation changes with disproportionate mechanical value: a limited magazine only becomes a decision when the player can inspect it.

Recovery now closes a similarly practical gap. The fresh defeat visibly reports 45 health, $40 debt and no lost cash or carried goods, without inventing a medical shipment. The explicit laden fixture lists $17 cash, $60 cargo, Mara's item and medicine, explains where to recover those goods, and retains Ivo's identification only in that relevant context. A loss screen should reconcile the inventory before it attempts to be moving. This one now does that competently.

## Verdict and limits

Eight for this increment, up from Police Response's seven. Collective firing, elapsed escape and readable ammunition answer the specific reservations that held that build back. The score does not rewrite Police Response or Demo03. No blocking defect was reproduced. My remaining reservation is the breadth of the choice: the demonstrated successful retreat is early and uninjured, while late stress behavior belongs to a deliberately abnormal durability fixture. A delayed or wounded escape and a normal-rule encounter with depleted officers would merit the next focused replay. I do not require a repeated identical full run to establish what this one already shows.

The measured stress section used an Intel Core i9-14900KF and NVIDIA RTX 4090, D3D11, 1280×720, capped at 30 FPS. It recorded 2,250 frames: p50 33.334 ms, p95 33.341 ms, max 43.106 ms, with 111,603,487 bytes reported by Unity's allocated-memory metric and one Funstra process. That supports the behavior of this capped run on this machine; it is not minimum-spec evidence, total process memory, or a human smoothness judgment. All gameplay was muted. Human enjoyment, input feel, difficulty and owner acceptance remain separate. No gameplay edits, build, packaging, publication or issue writes were performed by this reviewer.
