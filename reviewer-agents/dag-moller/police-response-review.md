## Review

The useful change is that shooting a resident now belongs to the same town as the police. The route creates a real player projectile, establishes that the resident remains unharmed at the trigger, then advances the projectile into an injury and a reported gunman. It subsequently releases the normal update loop and an officer spends ammunition to wound the exposed player. That is a respectable causal seam. The player can attack someone, but the town is no longer merely a collection of unrelated targets.

I am more interested in the trucks than their silhouette suggests. Six people are accounted for across two deliveries; saving a requested dispatch does not produce a squad instantly, and saving a casualty does not quietly replace them. The fixture reconstructs two trucks and six reinforcement records, with one casualty leaving eight living police. The deployed screenshot actually says eight. Numbers that agree across logistics, casualties and the interface are worth having. Source inspection also shows a blocked truck stopping to unload through checked exits, not passing through the obstruction to preserve a scheduled entrance. Those rules give positioning a chance to matter.

The distinction between a sound and an identification is equally necessary. The explicit report fixture shows a noise search without a named gunman, and moving the hidden player does not move the stored report. All three patrols receive the identified report. Search expiry and a subsequent unidentified noise do not activate an old, pending truck. This is competent knowledge bookkeeping. It does not, by itself, prove that escape feels readable in a running street fight: the route directly advances the expiry clock, and the HUD offers a response count without showing the age or location of the last report. I would like the player to understand why the pursuit continues before being asked to infer a 45-second rule from a manual.

The important reservation is scale. The logistics section deliberately omits combat, repeatedly refreshes reports and explicitly steps production functions. The live exchange then starts a fresh three-patrol setup and stops after the first wound. I saw evidence of a finite nine-person roster and evidence of live return fire. I did not see nine police fighting together. The deployed frame shows officers gathered beside their trucks; a paused frame cannot establish whether they subsequently spread out, obstruct one another or create distinct dangerous firing lanes. Do not sell that as coordinated maximum pressure yet.

There is also a deliberate exhaustion point. The reinforcement crew has finite ammunition and cannot be replenished after both truck records are spent until recovery resets the response. That is an honest boundary for this increment, but it makes ammunition baiting an obvious next experiment. It may be a good player option. It may turn the response into an arithmetic waiting exercise. Neither verdict follows from the current short firefight. I prefer this explicit limitation to an invisible infinite reserve, and want the next slice to give the limitation an interesting consequence.

## Verdict and continuity

Seven for this increment. The response adds useful interacting rules and tangible arrivals, and the inspected checks agree with the visible living-officer count. UI friction and the untested collective fight keep this below the level at which I would call its pressure model established. No blocking defect was reproduced in my run.

My Demo03 eight remains unchanged. That review primarily judged medicine, refuge and traffic conservation; this score concerns a narrower new enforcement slice. I did not independently review Demo04, and cannot claim to have closed the old long-session economy questions with a police fixture. The continuity is methodological: people and magazines now deserve the same accounting as medicine. The next replay should test all nine officers in a live encounter, an actual escape with elapsed search time, and the result of exhausting their ammunition. Rifles, grenades and 30-plus army deployment remain future work.

All gameplay was muted. The log identifies D3D11 on an RTX 4090; a 30 FPS cap is not minimum-spec validation, and I make no human listening, input-feel or sustained performance claim.
