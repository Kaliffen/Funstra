# The city of Funstra

World foundation approved 7 September 2026; geography developed by the CD on 8 September 2026. Companion to [VISION.md](VISION.md). This is setting intent; [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md) distinguishes the published build from future implementation.

## Before the debt

Funstra was a free port at the mouth of an inland river. Its wealth came from moving what other places could make but could not move safely: grain, machines, medicines and people. Dock unions, merchants and municipal engineers argued over it for centuries. Nobody quite owned it.

Eighty-three years ago, storm surges, crop failures and interrupted trade became known as the Salt Years. The lower city drowned. The national government stopped paying its employees and eventually stopped answering. Refugees arrived on dredgers. Hospitals ran out of clean blood. The pumps kept running because their crews slept beside them.

Those who kept Funstra alive formed the Emergency Compact: dock labor, medical colleges, shipping houses and the surviving city administration. They raised seawalls, converted warehouses into homes, and issued relief credit for food, shelter and treatment. Future municipal revenues were supposed to repay it.

The emergency ended. The credit system did not.

Shipping houses bought the relief debt. Temporary accommodation became rent. Medical bills could attach to work permits. Inherited obligations kept families in the same factories for generations. Citizenship became a ledger entry administered by the institutions collecting the debt. The Compact still calls this reconstruction.

## The present fault line

The flood defenses are failing again. Repair requires money, skilled labor and access through Old Port. Creditor houses propose clearing the district and relocating its residents into company housing. Harbor workers can block that plan but depend on the same houses for wages and imported food. Clinics buy supplies through relief contracts. Officers enforce orders that can displace their own relatives.

You arrive during that dispute. Nobody has enough authority to settle it cleanly. Small organizations can matter because powerful institutions depend on people they treat as replaceable.

There is no single secret explaining every wrong. The disaster was real, reconstruction achieved real things, and exploitation grew inside that achievement. People disagree about what to preserve, abolish or take for themselves.

## What makes the city strange

The medical colleges engineered survival as enthusiastically as harbor engineers rebuilt seawalls. Filter grafts let salvage divers work in polluted floodwater. Toughened skin protected chemical laborers. Cultured tissue replaced lost body parts. Their descendants and imitators are ordinary city residents.

Machines still carry the markings of public services that no longer exist. A hereditary pump crew's ritual calendar encodes an obsolete service manual. A noodle shop grows protein in tanks under the dining room. A salvage worker breathes where an officer needs a mask, but pays for treatment to tolerate dry air through a long shift.

The tone is maritime industrial decay, resourceful neighborhood life and institutional absurdity. Warm kitchens, ridiculous bureaucracy, loyalty and petty vanity belong beside coercion and injury. People have reasons to like living here. Oddness appears in bodies, work, food, equipment and beliefs before exposition.

## Old Port and Mara

Old Port is the first contested neighborhood in the redevelopment corridor. The arcade, garage, pawn counter, depot and waterfront already provide useful geography. Their owners, inventories and dependencies need to become meaningful.

Mara runs Pawn & Co. and finds work outside the licensed labor offices. She keeps people afloat and keeps excellent accounts. She believes personal obligations are more humane than institutional ones. Whether she lives by that distinction depends on whom she is dealing with.

You recently lost an employer-backed work permit. Mara advanced money for a bed and helped keep that loss out of the local registry. You owe her, but she did not save a prophesied hero: she backed someone who looked useful. This explains the existing favors without making them destiny.

Eventually, character creation should offer former occupations and ties. The common opening is precarity in Old Port; it prescribes neither politics nor eventual organization. The present opening retains one background; expand origins when occupations create distinct playable choices.

The courier bag, Vico's lockbox and depot ledger can connect to relief contracts and diverted goods. Their contents need not all be righteous contraband. Preserve the enjoyable route-and-escape pacing, then make choosing a recipient consequential. These consequences do not already exist in the demo.

## History translated into play

| World fact | Mechanical consequence to pursue |
|---|---|
| Citizenship is administered through obligations | Debt affects access and affiliation; stolen or corrected records matter beyond sale price. |
| Public functions are fragmented | Several groups can provide protection or treatment, creating different dependencies. |
| The city survives through movement of goods | Schedules, stores, wages and shortages generate conflict without endless quest dispensers. |
| Biological repair became an industry | Wounds, medical skills and later grafts connect survival to economic and social history. |
| Residents remember the earlier emergency | Beliefs and relationships influence collaboration, organizing, compliance and exploitation. |

Encounter these facts through a denied permit, an unusual body, an empty shelf or a request. Reading history must never be required to understand an immediate decision.


## The shape of Old Port

The old shoreline was a working edge before it became a defense line. Market lane grew on the dry approach to the quay; later sheds and bonded yards interrupted older public passages. Clinic court occupies converted relief rooms behind that lane. Its shared entrance explains why care, shelter and neighbors meet in one small place.

Goods move between the quay's landing stores, the bonded yard and inland customers. Public streets are visible and socially legible. Service passages are shorter but cross someone's workplace or locked boundary. Access is a relationship as well as a distance.

Beyond the quay, the pump approach carries workers and repair supplies along the raised flood defenses. The Harbor Combine wants dependable passage and control of relief contracts. Dock Mutual wants its crews and residents to retain access and bargaining power. Neither can keep the works running without labor, supplies and local cooperation. The College's clinic treats those workers; Mara makes a living in the gaps between official arrangements.

The coming local dispute concerns who bears the cost of keeping this access open, and who may exclude others once it is repaired. A player may support a shared service, extract tolls, exploit diverted supplies or make a modest living around the dispute. No chosen-person role is needed.

Preserve warmth: workers share meals, tenants improvise repairs, Tally finds occupied rooms. Mark flood heights on walls and old civic ownership on reused machinery. Put history in usable places and ordinary work before adding another explanatory speech.

## Weapons, the bureau and the people between

The Compact reserves armed force to authorized institutions and their licensed contractors. Civilian possession outside that authorization is illegal. Enforcement is uneven: a creditor's guard can carry openly where a dockworker risks confiscation. Ammunition passes through controlled imports, guarded stores and illicit dealers. Access depends on money, trust, debts and diverted shipments.

The Compact's investigative bureau follows offenses that cross local precincts or threaten its contracts: Funstra's counterpart to federal investigators, not the real-world FBI. Its agents need witnesses, records and cooperation. Local officers, employers and neighbors may help them, obstruct them or exploit their attention.

Lawyers navigate the Compact's overlapping contracts and enforcement powers. They have clients, fees and loyalties; representation can negotiate terms or challenge evidence without erasing an injury or an offended person's memory. Dealers, bosses and helpful contacts likewise depend on specific people and places. Betrayal can deliver profit or protection, but only discovered facts can inform another person's response.
