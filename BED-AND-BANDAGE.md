# A Bed & a Bandage — Demo 02 review guide

Version 0.2.0, 7 September 2026. A playable first step from isolated survivor to a dependable partnership. This is the next development demo following Hot Cargo, not a finished sandbox campaign.

## The situation

Neri runs a curbside clinic in Old Port. Ivo has impounded six medical doses against its debt, with Rook guarding the case in Vico's alley. Ivo will release it for $100. After twelve minutes of active simulation, an unpaid shipment is sold to a buyer and moved to the north quay; release then costs $140. Reading menus and tactical pause stop the clock.

You can collect released goods, steal while the guard looks away, fight for access, keep the stock, give it to Neri, or sell it through Mara for $160. These approaches transfer the same six doses. Donation and sale cannot both pay out.

The clinic starts with two doses. Every ninety seconds, while supplies and patient funds permit, it uses one dose and receives $8. Every four minutes it can buy two doses for $30 from a finite reserve of four. Empty shelves suspend treatment. The initial twelve doses are conserved across stockpiles, deliveries and consumption. This is a deliberately small economic simulation, not a citywide one.

The buyer sale and the clinic's transactions are scheduled state changes. This build does not animate a delivery truck or each patient's treatment.

## Your first power reward

Delivering the medicine earns three trust with Neri. Ask them to join. They follow you, can hold or retreat to the clinic, and use their own limited dressings to stabilize you. They can be wounded and incapacitated; bring a bandage back to help them. At the clinic you can rest, receive treatment and convert a dose into two dressings for Neri.

Neri provides medical support; they are not an autonomous attacker and cannot yet be directly piloted. The reward is a practical partnership, not ownership of the neighborhood. Hurting Neri breaks that relationship. If the medicine goes elsewhere, the solo life and the original activities remain playable.

## Combat and consequences

RMB selects a person. LMB attacks them from the central world view. Fists have a short reach; the pistol requires ammunition and an unobstructed shot within eighteen meters. Rook returns fire with a finite magazine and searches a last-seen location instead of shooting through walls. Space pauses combat for inspection and companion orders.

Residents and officers share damage/incapacitation and stabilization rules; their wounds persist. Police retain the prototype pursuit/arrest behavior. There is no reinforcement economy or character death in this demo.

Bleeding drains health until bandaged or treated. A bandage stabilizes and restores a little health; clinic treatment consumes stock and fully heals. Defeat costs up to $30 and loose goods. A nearby, able Neri improves recovery and avoids the $40 emergency-bed debt. Otherwise a credit-funded bed preserves a way back into play. Resting at home can bring a wounded solo player to 80 health for another $40 debt, even if penniless and the medical shipment has already been sold.

A missing-stock audit does not automatically identify you. Being seen taking the stock or attacking the collector's people does. That identified grievance survives the immediate chase and reloading. Ivo accepts $60 restitution to withdraw Rook's standing hostility. Police pursuit and Ivo's grievance are separate systems.

## Sessions and cargo

F5 manually saves. Autosaves, transactions, opening the pause menu and normal quitting also checkpoint state. Loose cargo, medical stock, current story goods, wounds, crew, positions and knowledge survive resuming this demo. Extraction progress itself resets, so resume the hold when you return. There is no progress while the application is closed.

Original purple cargo remains a repeatable activity with immediate restocking on bank/arrest. Five or more load reduces movement speed by 20%; the HUD says so. The satchel raises capacity from six to nine. That old supply behavior is intentionally distinct from finite medical stock during this transition.

## Suggested playtest

For a fresh impression, choose a new night rather than importing an experienced character. Spend the opening minutes following your own curiosity before reading the routes below.

Try different outcomes across separate new nights:

1. Earn $100 from Mara or cargo, pay Ivo, collect the case, give it to Neri and recruit them. Get hurt, retreat and see whether their aid changes the experience.
2. Watch Rook. Take the case without being seen. Compare the later stock-discovery report with a witnessed theft.
3. Use combat to obtain the same case. Break police sight, check the journal and return to Ivo later. Test whether the lasting consequence is understandable.
4. Sell the medicine through Mara, or let the buyer appointment pass. Revisit the clinic and inspect what actually changed.
5. Save or quit while carrying goods or wounded, resume, and try a cargo extraction interrupted by movement, key release, pause and heat.

These are prompts, not required quests or a scoring rubric. No named reviewer has yet completed a human acceptance pass for this build.

## What feedback will decide

- Did you feel a new capability when Neri joined, or merely receive another companion icon?
- Did you understand who needed the medicine and why? Was there a reason to care beyond the reward?
- Did watching, positioning, ammunition and injury change a decision during play?
- Were the collector's knowledge, pursuit and restitution fair and legible?
- Could you stop a session comfortably and return without losing your place?
- Where did you become bored, confused, stuck or dominant enough that the choices stopped mattering?

Please include the approach taken, approximate active playtime, resolution/hardware, and any reproduction steps for bugs. Especially useful: a short account of an unexpected event and what you think caused it.
